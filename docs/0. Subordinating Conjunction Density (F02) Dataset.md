## 3. Operational Feature Definition (F02: Subordinating Conjunction Density)

*Extracted directly from `static/artifacts/4. Enhanced Discourse Lead Features.md`*

### Short Description
This feature measures the functional deployment of subordinating conjunctions to construct complex syntactic relationships that frame arguments through causality, concession, or conditional dependency. A "True" ($1$) classification requires the use of at least one subordinating conjunction to logically bridge two distinct propositions, thereby signaling the writer’s attempt to articulate reasoning beyond simple coordination.

---

### Operational Presence Rules & Criteria

#### Presence Condition (True / $F02 = 1$)
The feature is marked as **True** ($1$) when the text contains at least one subordinating conjunction that functions to subordinate a dependent clause to a main clause, specifically to:
* **Establish Causality:** Explaining *why* a phenomenon occurs (e.g., *because*, *since*, *as*).
* **Introduce Concession:** Acknowledging an opposing viewpoint or limitation before asserting the primary claim (e.g., *although*, *despite*, *while*).
* **Define Conditional Constraints:** Establishing a logical framework or "if/then" requirement for an argument (e.g., *if*, *provided that*, *unless*).

#### Key Linguistic Markers
* **Causal/Explanatory:** *Because, since, as, given that.*
* **Concessive/Contrastive:** *Although, even though, while, whereas, despite* (used in a subordinate structure).
* **Conditional/Hypothetical:** *If, provided that, unless, whether* (when functioning as a conditional determiner).

#### Exclusion Boundaries (False / $F02 = 0$)
The feature is **False** ($0$) if:
* **Correlative/Coordinating usage only:** The text uses only coordinating conjunctions (FANBOYS: *for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so*) to link independent clauses without creating true subordination.
* **Superficial/Punctuation Errors:** The marker appears as a sentence fragment or is used in a non-rhetorical, "filler" capacity that fails to bridge two distinct logical propositions (e.g., "Although the weather, I went out").
* **Lexical Ambiguity:** The term appears as a preposition or adverb rather than a conjunction connecting clauses (e.g., "Since 1980..." where *since* acts as a preposition indicating time, not a logical link between arguments).

---

### Illustrative Student Lead Examples

* **Example 1 (Concession):**
  > "Venus is planet that scientists have trying to find out more about **although** the planet is very hostile and dangerous."
  * *Rationale:* Uses *although* to effectively subordinate a clause of concession, tempering the previous claim about research with the reality of the planet's hostility.

* **Example 2 (Causal/Reasoning):**
  > "I would disagree **because** aliens are not real and why would they just made a radom face into the ground."
  * *Rationale:* Employs *because* to establish a clear causal link between the writer's position (disagreement) and the evidence (the absurdity of the alien theory).

* **Example 3 (Conditional):**
  > "**If** scientists can figure out more about Venus, we can further our understanding of what this planet is and what it once was."
  * *Rationale:* Utilizes *if* to create a conditional logical framework, linking scientific inquiry (the *if*) to the potential advancement of knowledge (the *then*).
